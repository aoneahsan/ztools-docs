# GSC Mastery

It connects to Search Console through your own read-only Google OAuth and uses your own LLM key (BYOK), both encrypted per user, so the AI reasoning bills to your provider, not to a shared pool. Point it at a property and it surfaces keyword gaps, position drops, rising and winning queries, and page-level opportunities, then suggests concrete next moves. ## Use cases

### Diagnose a sudden traffic drop

After your weekly traffic dips, you open GSC Mastery and run the position-drop analysis on the last 28 days versus the prior period. Instead of scrolling thousands of rows, you get a ranked list of queries and URLs that slipped, grouped by likely cause, plus prompts to check whether a template change or a competitor’s new page is involved. You verify the flagged URLs against the raw report before acting.

### Find striking-distance keywords

A content lead wants quick wins for next quarter. They run the keyword-gap and rising-query prompts to pull queries sitting in positions 8 to 20 with real impression volume, where a title rewrite or an added FAQ section could push a page onto page one. The tool clusters them by topic and existing URL, so the lead leaves with a short, prioritized list of pages to refresh rather than a raw spreadsheet.

### Resolve a page cannibalization question

An SEO consultant managing a client’s blog suspects two articles compete for the same query. They run the page-level opportunity prompt for that query and get a side-by-side of which URL Google prefers across impressions, clicks, and average position, plus a suggested consolidation or canonical move. They take the read-out into the client call as a plain-English explanation rather than a wall of metrics.

### Run a monthly winning-queries review

A solo founder closes each month by running the winning-queries and rising-query analyses to see what gained ground without paid effort. The output highlights queries and pages trending up, with notes on which to double down on. Because it uses their own OpenAI or Anthropic key, the cost shows on their own LLM bill, and they keep the export to compare against next month’s run.

## How it works

1. **Connect Search Console** — Sign in with your own read-only Google OAuth and pick a verified Search Console property. The connection is scoped to read your performance data only, and the credential is encrypted and stored per user.
2. **Add your LLM key** — Paste your own LLM provider key, such as OpenAI or Anthropic. It is encrypted per user and powers the analysis, so the reasoning tokens bill to your account, not a shared ZTools pool.
3. **Choose an analysis and range** — Pick a prebuilt prompt — keyword gaps, position drops, rising or winning queries, or page-level opportunities — and set the date range and property you want it to read.
4. **Let it pull and interpret** — The tool fetches your Search Console rows for that range and feeds them to your LLM with the chosen prompt. You get a plain-English read-out of patterns and suggested next moves.
5. **Verify, then act** — Cross-check the flagged queries and URLs against the raw Search Console report, then apply the suggested moves you agree with. Export or save the run to compare against future analyses.

<details>
<summary>**Why might the tool miss some of my queries?**</summary>

Because it inherits Google Search Console’s own limits. GSC returns roughly the top 1,000 rows per query, keeps about a 16-month window, and omits anonymized low-volume queries entirely. GSC Mastery can only analyze the data Search Console hands over, so very long-tail terms and older history may not appear. For exhaustive row-level work, pair it with a direct GSC or BigQuery export.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What data does it access, and where does it go?**</summary>

It requests read-only access to your Search Console performance data through Google OAuth — it cannot change your property or submit anything. Your OAuth and LLM keys are encrypted per user. When you run an analysis, the relevant rows are sent to your chosen LLM provider for interpretation, so your data does pass through that provider under your own account and their terms.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Which analyses are included?**</summary>

The prebuilt prompts cover keyword gaps, position drops, rising queries, winning queries, and page-level opportunities. Each takes your selected property and date range, reads the matching Search Console data, and returns a plain-English summary with suggested next moves. You pick the analysis that matches your question rather than writing prompts from scratch.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Does it guarantee better rankings?**</summary>

No. It explains what your Search Console data shows and suggests moves a human SEO might make next, but it cannot promise a ranking change. Rankings depend on competitors, content quality, links, and Google’s algorithm — none of which the tool controls. It is a faster way to read your own data and form a hypothesis, not a guarantee of results.

</details>

## Tips

- Compare matching periods (28 days vs the prior 28, or year-over-year) so position-drop and rising-query analyses reflect real movement, not different sample sizes.
- Run analyses per directory or per template (e.g. /blog vs /docs) so the AI clusters issues that share a root cause instead of blending unrelated pages.
- Set a spending limit on your LLM provider key — since usage bills to your account, a cap prevents a large date range from running up unexpected token costs.
- Keep each run’s export; comparing this month’s winning-queries output to last month’s is a cheap way to track whether your fixes actually moved impressions.
- When the tool flags a query, open it in raw GSC filtered to that query and page before acting — anonymized and sub-1,000-row data won’t be in the AI’s view.
- Use a cheaper, faster model for routine monthly scans and switch to a stronger model only for deep diagnosis, since you control which model your key calls.
