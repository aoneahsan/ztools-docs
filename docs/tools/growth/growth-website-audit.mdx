## Use cases

### Pre-publish check for a new blog post

Before hitting publish on a long-form guide, paste the staging URL to confirm the title tag, meta description, single H1, heading order, image alt text, and internal links are all in place. The AEO card flags whether your intro answers the target question in the first sentence — the part answer engines tend to quote — so you fix structure before the post goes live, not weeks after.

### Triage a page that ranks but isn’t cited by AI

Your product comparison page sits on page one of Google yet never shows up in ChatGPT or Perplexity answers. Run the GEO and AEO cards to see whether the page lacks an FAQ block, clear definitions, or extractable Q&A passages. The fix-first list tells you which passage-level changes to make so the content becomes quotable, not just rankable.

### Hand a client a clear before/after

As a freelancer auditing a small-business homepage, generate one report you can screenshot into a proposal: three scores plus a numbered list of concrete fixes like ’add meta description,’ ’compress hero image,’ or ’add Organization schema.’ You skip the manual checklist and give the client plain priorities instead of a 40-tab spreadsheet they will never open.

### Catch technical regressions after a redeploy

After a theme update or framework migration, audit your key landing pages to catch silent breakages — a canonical pointing at staging, a noindex left on, a meta description truncated by a CMS field limit, or new render-blocking markup. Because the scan reads the live page, it surfaces what actually shipped rather than what the CMS preview promised.

## How it works

1. **Enter a URL** — Paste any public page URL into the audit field and start the scan. The tool fetches the live HTML and technical signals it can read from that single page, without needing access to your analytics or server.
2. **On-page SEO analysis** — It checks title tag, meta description, heading hierarchy, image alt text, canonical, robots directives, Open Graph tags, and internal linking, scoring each signal and noting what is missing or misconfigured.
3. **AEO scoring** — The page is evaluated for answer-engine readiness: definition-first intros, FAQ blocks, scannable headings, and structured Q&A passages that AI assistants can extract and quote when responding to a user question.
4. **GEO scoring** — It assesses generative-engine visibility signals — schema coverage, clarity, citable passages, and content depth — that influence whether systems like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews surface your page.
5. **Prioritized fix list** — Findings are merged into one fix-first checklist ordered by likely impact, so you start with the changes most likely to move all three scores instead of guessing where to begin.

AEO (answer-engine optimization) measures how easily an AI assistant can extract and quote your page when answering a question. GEO (generative-engine optimization) measures broader visibility across generative systems like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and AI Overviews. The tool scores all three separately.

## Tips

- Lead every key page with a one-sentence literal definition of its topic — answer engines often quote the first sentence as the answer block.
- Add an FAQ section with genuine questions and concise answers; it tends to lift the AEO and GEO cards the most for content pages.
- Re-run the audit right after a redeploy or CMS migration to catch silent regressions like a stray noindex or a canonical pointing at staging.
- Fix in the order the list gives you — it is sorted by likely impact, so the top items usually move more than one score at once.
- Pair this with off-site work like backlinks, mentions, and an llms.txt file; the audit covers on-page and technical signals only, so it cannot see your link profile.
