## Use cases

### Planning a blog cluster around one topic

You’re about to write a guide on ’sourdough starter’ and need angles you haven’t thought of. Enter the seed term and the tool expands it into phrases like ’sourdough starter not rising,’ ’sourdough starter discard recipes,’ and ’sourdough starter day 3.’ Each becomes a subheading or its own post, so your cluster covers the questions people actually search instead of guessing.

### Finding buyer phrasing for product pages

Running an online store for hiking gear, you expand ’trail running shoes’ and see modifiers like ’for wide feet,’ ’for flat feet,’ and ’waterproof.’ These reveal how shoppers describe their needs, so you can name collections and write product copy in their words — matching real intent rather than the generic ’best running shoes’ phrasing every competitor already targets.

### Sourcing video titles and FAQ sections

Before filming a tutorial, you expand ’excel pivot table’ and collect autocomplete phrases such as ’pivot table not refreshing,’ ’pivot table calculated field,’ and ’pivot table from multiple sheets.’ You turn the most common ones into chapter markers and an on-page FAQ, so the video and its description answer the specific questions viewers type — improving the odds of surfacing for those searches.

### Briefing a landing page from a service term

A bookkeeping agency expands ’small business accounting’ and uncovers ’for ecommerce,’ ’software for self-employed,’ and ’near me’ variations. These become headline tests and FAQ entries for a new service page, so the copy speaks to the exact situations prospects describe — and the team gets a ranked starting list to validate against a paid volume tool before committing budget.

## How it works

1. **Enter a seed keyword** — Type a single topic or phrase — like ’meal prep’ or ’react hooks’ — into the search box and choose your target market’s language and region if prompted.
2. **The tool queries Google Suggest** — It sends your seed to the free public Google Suggest endpoint — no API key required — and gathers the autocomplete completions Google returns for that prefix.
3. **Expand into long-tail variations** — It appends letters and question words to your seed (a-z, who, what, how, best, vs) and re-queries, multiplying one term into dozens of real long-tail suggestions.
4. **Group and score by heuristic difficulty** — Results are clustered by theme and tagged with a relative difficulty signal derived from phrase length and modifiers — a directional hint, not a verified keyword-difficulty score.
5. **Review, copy, or export** — Scan the grouped ideas, copy the phrases worth pursuing, and carry them into your content brief or a paid volume tool to confirm hard search numbers.

## Tips

- Start broad, then re-seed: take a promising long-tail result and feed it back in as a new seed to mine a second layer of even more specific phrases.
- Watch the question-word groups (how, what, why, can) — these map directly to FAQ entries and featured-snippet opportunities.
- Treat ’vs’ and ’alternative’ suggestions as comparison-page briefs; they signal high commercial intent worth a dedicated page.
- Run the same seed in each target locale before translating content — search phrasing rarely maps one-to-one between languages.
- Always pair the heuristic difficulty with a real volume check in Ahrefs or Semrush before committing budget — the tool finds the idea, not the forecast.
- Export your shortlist early so you can dedupe across multiple seeds and spot the phrases that keep recurring — those are your cluster pillars.
