## Use cases

### Pre-meeting investor sanity check

A seed-stage founder is about to send a fundraising deck and wants to know what an investor will see if they ask ChatGPT about the company. They run AI Brand Visibility, read how each engine summarizes the product and which sources it cites, and spot that Perplexity is pulling an outdated tagline from a stale press release, so they fix the source before the meeting.

### Category comparison for content planning

A marketing lead for a project-management SaaS turns on the competitor benchmark and asks all four engines to compare her product against two named rivals. She gets each model’s framing of strengths and gaps side by side, notices Gemini omits her free tier entirely, and uses that gap to brief the content team on which comparison page to publish next.

### Reputation monitoring after an incident

After a public outage, a support manager checks whether the AI engines are repeating a negative narrative. He reads the sentiment on each answer and the citations behind it, finds that Claude still references a months-old complaint thread, and prioritizes a fresh status-page post to give the models a more current source to draw from.

### Monthly agency reporting

A boutique agency runs the tool monthly for a retail client across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity, exports each answer with its citations, and pastes the side-by-side into a client report. Because results are a snapshot, they label every capture with its date and treat shifts between months as directional signal rather than a scoreboard.

## How it works

1. **Add your API key** — Paste at least an OpenAI key (and optionally Gemini, Claude, or Perplexity keys) into your encrypted vault; keys are wrapped per-user with HKDF and AES-GCM and never shared with anyone.
2. **Enter your brand and prompts** — Give the tool your brand name and the questions you care about, such as ’What is [brand]?’ or ’Is [brand] trustworthy?’, plus any competitors you want benchmarked alongside it.
3. **Run the engines in parallel** — The tool fires your prompts at the selected AI chat engines at the same time, so you get a full multi-engine read in one pass instead of querying each provider manually one by one.
4. **Read answers, citations, and sentiment** — Each engine returns its description with the sources it cited and a sentiment read, laid out side by side so you can compare framing across models and spot stale or incorrect sources fast.
5. **Save the snapshot and re-run later** — Export or store the capture with its date, then run it again next week or month; because model output shifts over time, comparing dated snapshots shows you the direction of change.

## Tips

- Run the same prompt set on a fixed cadence — weekly or monthly — and always label captures with their date, so you are comparing dated snapshots rather than reacting to normal model variance.
- Add keys for all four engines, not just OpenAI, to catch cases where one model is carrying a stale source the others have already dropped.
- Read the citations before the answer text; a wrong description almost always traces back to one outdated source you can refresh on your own site.
- Use neutral, literal prompts (’What is [brand]?’, ’Is [brand] reliable?’) that mirror how real users ask, instead of leading questions that bias the model’s framing.
- Turn the competitor benchmark on sparingly — each added rival multiplies the queries that bill to your provider account, so benchmark only the two or three names that actually matter.
