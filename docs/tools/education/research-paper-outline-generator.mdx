# Research Paper Outline Generator

A research-paper outline generator produces a sectioned skeleton for an academic paper based on the paper's type — empirical / IMRaD (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion), argumentative essay, literature review, theoretical / conceptual paper — populated with the standard sub-sections expected in each, so writers move from blank page to structured draft in minutes. The ZTools Research Paper Outline Generator runs entirely in the browser, lets the user pick paper type + working title + research question(s), and outputs a hierarchical outline with section headings, recommended word counts per section, and per-section guidance prompts that act as a self-coaching checklist.

## Use cases

### Empirical study draft (IMRaD)

Pick "IMRaD"; tool produces Introduction → Methods (Participants / Materials / Procedure / Analysis) → Results → Discussion → References. Recommended word counts per section anchor pacing.

### Literature review

Pick "Literature review"; outline includes Search strategy → Theoretical framework → Themes → Synthesis → Gaps & future work. Avoids the common error of summarising sources without synthesis.

### Argumentative essay

Pick "Argumentative"; outline gives Thesis → Background → Argument 1 (Claim / Evidence / Warrant) → Counter-argument → Rebuttal → Conclusion. Forces engagement with opposing view.

### Conference paper draft

8-12 page conference paper template: Abstract → Intro → Related work → Approach → Experiments → Discussion → Conclusion → References. Per-section word budgets fit standard page limits.

## How it works

1. **Pick paper type** — IMRaD (empirical), argumentative, literature review, theoretical / conceptual, conference paper.
2. **Provide working title + research question** — These seed the outline with topic-aware section prompts (e.g. "discuss your hypothesis: …").
3. **Generate hierarchical outline** — Tool produces top-level sections + sub-sections + per-section guidance text + recommended word count.
4. **Customise** — Add / remove / rename sections; reorder; expand sub-sections. Save and re-open in browser storage.
5. **Export** — Markdown, plain text, or Word-friendly formatting. Paste into your editor; outline becomes scaffolding, not output.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Is the outline a substitute for actually researching?**</summary>

No — it scaffolds. You still do the literature search, design the study, run the analysis. The outline tells you where each output goes.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What is IMRaD?**</summary>

Introduction → Methods → Results → Discussion. The dominant structure in empirical sciences (psychology, biology, medicine, engineering). Used for ~80% of journal articles in those fields.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How accurate are the word-count recommendations?**</summary>

Heuristics derived from typical published papers in each genre. Adjust to your venue's actual page / word limits. Most journals publish target lengths in their author guidelines.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I use this for a thesis chapter?**</summary>

Yes — the IMRaD or theoretical-paper outline scales up. A thesis is essentially a long IMRaD + extended literature review. Run the generator per chapter.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I avoid section-summary paralysis?**</summary>

Treat the outline as a skeleton, not a script. Start writing the section you have the most material for; outline lives in the background.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Should I send this outline to my advisor?**</summary>

Often yes — early-stage outlines surface assumptions about scope and methodology that are easier to fix before you write 5000 words.

</details>

## Tips

- Drafting follows the outline; revising often re-orders it. Plan to revisit the outline at least once after the first complete draft.
- Word-count budgets surface scope creep early — if Methods is consuming twice its budget, the experiment may be too complex.
- Write the abstract last but draft the title early — both anchor the rest of the writing.
- For collaborative papers, share the outline first; co-authors agreeing on structure prevents painful late-stage rewrites.
- Save outlines per project; reusing a familiar IMRaD skeleton makes later papers faster to start.
