# macronutrient-calculator

A macronutrient calculator splits a daily calorie target into protein, carbohydrate, and fat targets based on goals, body composition, and training type — protein anchored by lean mass (1.6–2.2 g/kg for active people, higher during cuts), fat by minimum endocrine needs (~0.6–1.0 g/kg), and carbohydrates filling the remainder. The ZTools Macronutrient Calculator runs entirely in the browser, supports goal-specific protein recommendations (cut: 2.0–2.4 g/kg of lean body mass; maintenance: 1.6 g/kg; bulk: 1.8 g/kg), separates fat into "minimum healthy" vs "preferred", and surfaces the math so users see why each number lands where it does.

## Use cases

### Diet-phase macro targets

Someone in a 500 kcal cut needs higher protein than maintenance to preserve lean mass. Calculator shows the bumped protein target explicitly.

### Bulking macro targets

Lean bulks favour modest protein (1.8 g/kg) and abundant carbs to fuel training. Calculator suggests carbs as the largest macro by gram count.

### Endurance vs strength training

Endurance athletes lean carb-heavier; strength athletes protein-heavier. Calculator preset adapts.

### Educating clients about macros

Personal trainers explain "400 g of carbs" in context — what that means in real food (oats, rice, fruit).

## How it works

1. **Enter daily calories** — From the Calorie / TDEE Calculator, or set manually.
2. **Enter weight + body-fat % (optional)** — Lean body mass = weight × (1 − bf%). Used for protein targets.
3. **Pick goal** — Cut, maintain, bulk. Each adjusts protein and fat distribution.
4. **Compute** — Protein: g/kg × LBM (or weight if no body-fat). Fat: 25–35% of calories or 0.6–1.0 g/kg minimum, whichever is higher. Carbs: remainder.
5. **Convert to grams + meal split** — Per-meal split for 3–6 meals/day. Easier to plan than one daily total.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**How much protein do I really need?**</summary>

For sedentary adults, ~0.8 g/kg/day. For active people, 1.6 g/kg. For cutting (preserve lean mass under deficit), 2.2–2.4 g/kg of LBM. Above 2.5 g/kg shows diminishing returns.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can fat be too low?**</summary>

Yes — sub-0.6 g/kg risks hormone disruption (testosterone, oestrogen, vitamin absorption). Default minimum is 25% of calories, never below 50 g/day for adults.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Are carbs essential?**</summary>

Not strictly — the body produces glucose from protein and fat. But athletic performance benefits from carbs, and most people sustain diets better with them. Low-carb / keto is a personal choice, not a metabolic mandate.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Why use lean body mass for protein and not total weight?**</summary>

Adipose tissue is metabolically less protein-demanding. For very lean people, total weight ≈ LBM, so the difference is small. For higher body-fat individuals, LBM-based targets prevent over-prescribing.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Should I eat the same macros every day?**</summary>

For most goals, daily consistency is fine. Some athletes "carb cycle" (high on training days, low on rest) — works for some, not necessary for most.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I hit these numbers?**</summary>

Track for 2–4 weeks via an app. Most users vastly under- or over-shoot protein without tracking. After learning, eyeballing becomes accurate.

</details>

## Tips

- Hit protein first; fat as a secondary minimum; carbs fill the remainder.
- Spread protein across 3–5 meals — synthesis benefits from regular distribution.
- For cuts, hold protein high — losing muscle on a deficit is the #1 mistake.
- For bulks, hold fat at 25–30% — going too high "just because calories" hurts food volume / satiety.
- Track a few weeks until estimation feels natural — then loosen.
