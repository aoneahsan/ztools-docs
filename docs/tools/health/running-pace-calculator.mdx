# running-pace-calculator

A running pace calculator solves the pace × time × distance triangle — given any two of the three, it computes the third — supports both metric (min/km, km/h) and imperial (min/mi, mph), generates an even-pace split table for any race distance, and offers a target-time mode where you set a goal and see the pace required for each split. The ZTools Running Pace Calculator runs entirely in the browser, supports common race distances (5K, 10K, half marathon, marathon, ultras), and is the right tool for race-day pacing strategies, training-zone pace targets, and treadmill speed conversions.

## Use cases

### Race-day pacing strategy

A 4:00 marathon means 5:42/km. The pace table shows splits at every 5K so you adjust mid-race if drifting.

### Training pace targets

A 4:30 5K equates to ~5:55/km tempo pace. Use the calculator to derive long-run, tempo, threshold paces from your race results.

### Treadmill speed conversion

Treadmill shows km/h or mph; coaches think in min/km. Convert quickly between the two.

### Split-time race planning

Print an even-pace splits card for race day so each km / mile marker confirms you are on schedule.

## How it works

1. **Pick two known values** — Distance + finish time (compute pace). Distance + pace (compute time). Pace + time (compute distance).
2. **Enter values** — Pace as min:sec per km/mi. Time as h:mm:ss. Distance as km, mi, or named race (5K, 10K, half, full).
3. **Compute** — Returns the missing value plus equivalent pace/speed in alternate units.
4. **Generate splits** — For races, optionally generate a kilometre-by-kilometre or mile-by-mile splits table at the target pace.
5. **Compare paces** — Optional "what-if" mode: see how 10 sec/km faster changes your finish time.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Should I race even-paced or negative-split?**</summary>

Negative-splitting (faster second half) is the most successful strategy for most runners — start ~3–5 sec/km slower than goal, settle into goal pace, finish faster. Positive-splitting (slower second half) is the most common but produces worse finish times.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How does heat affect pace?**</summary>

Significantly — for every 5°C above ~15°C, expect ~2–4% slower pace at the same effort. Adjust race-day expectations accordingly.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What is "tempo pace"?**</summary>

Roughly 30 sec/km slower than 5K race pace, or "comfortably hard" — a pace you could sustain for 20–40 minutes. Used for lactate-threshold training.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do hills change pacing?**</summary>

On steep climbs, expect 30–60 sec/km slower for the same effort. The calculator assumes flat; adjust per course profile.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What pace should I run easy runs at?**</summary>

60–90 sec/km slower than 5K race pace. If 5K pace is 4:30/km, easy runs sit at 5:30–6:00/km.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I trust GPS watch pace?**</summary>

In open areas, yes — within 2%. Under tree cover or in cities with tall buildings, GPS error inflates. Lap-pace vs instant-pace also differ; lap-average is more reliable.

</details>

## Tips

- Print the splits card and tape to your wrist for race day — easier than doing math while running.
- Plan negative-splits — 3–5 sec/km slower start typically beats even-pace finish time.
- For hot races, plan a slower goal — pushing through heat costs more than pacing wisely.
- Use the calculator to derive training paces from your most recent race result.
- Recompute monthly — pace targets shift as fitness changes.
