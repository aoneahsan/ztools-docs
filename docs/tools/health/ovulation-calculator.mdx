# ovulation-calculator

An ovulation calculator estimates the most fertile days in a menstrual cycle — typically a 6-day window ending on the day of ovulation, with peak fertility 1–2 days before ovulation — by combining the average cycle length with the last menstrual period start date and applying the rule that ovulation occurs ~14 days before the next expected period. The ZTools Ovulation Calculator runs entirely in the browser, supports cycles from 21 to 40 days, surfaces the assumptions explicitly (regular cycles, predictable luteal phase), and is for general planning / education only — for medical fertility tracking, BBT charting, LH testing, and provider guidance are the more reliable signals.

## Use cases

### Conception planning

Couples trying to conceive identify the fertile window so timed intercourse increases pregnancy probability.

### Cycle awareness for general health

Tracking ovulation helps interpret cycle-related symptoms (mood, energy, sleep, cramping).

### Natural family planning

Some couples use cycle awareness as part of family planning, ideally combined with BBT and cervical-mucus tracking for accuracy. Not as effective as medical contraception.

### Travel and event scheduling

Anticipating period and ovulation timing helps plan around predictable cycle events.

## How it works

1. **Enter LMP date** — First day of the last menstrual period.
2. **Enter cycle length** — Average days from one period start to the next. Most cycles are 25–32 days.
3. **Compute ovulation day** — Next period date − 14 days = ovulation. (Luteal phase length is the more constant part of the cycle.)
4. **Compute fertile window** — 5 days before ovulation through the day of ovulation. Sperm survive ~5 days in the female reproductive tract; egg survives ~24 hours.
5. **Predict next 3 cycles** — Roll the calculation forward by cycle length each time. Useful for planning.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**How accurate is the calendar method?**</summary>

For regular 28-day cycles, reasonable. For irregular cycles, less so. The "luteal phase = 14 days" assumption holds for most people but varies between 12 and 16. Real ovulation is best confirmed by LH tests, BBT, or ultrasound.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Why is the fertile window 6 days, not 1?**</summary>

Sperm can fertilise the egg for up to 5 days after intercourse; the egg lives ~24 hours. So intercourse on any of the 5 days preceding ovulation, or the day of ovulation itself, can result in pregnancy.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I use this as contraception?**</summary>

Calendar-only methods have a typical-use failure rate of ~24% per year. Combined with BBT and cervical-mucus observation, that drops, but still much higher than hormonal methods, IUDs, or condoms. Talk to a clinician.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Why does my real ovulation differ from the prediction?**</summary>

Cycles vary cycle-to-cycle by 1–3 days. Stress, illness, travel, sleep changes shift ovulation. Confirm with LH tests for precision.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is this medical advice?**</summary>

No — general estimate only. For fertility issues, see a reproductive specialist.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can ovulation occur outside the predicted window?**</summary>

Yes. Stress and lifestyle changes can move ovulation by days. Some cycles produce no ovulation at all (anovulatory).

</details>

## Tips

- Track cycles for 3+ months to know your average length before relying on predictions.
- For conception, target days 1–2 before predicted ovulation for highest probability.
- Combine calendar method with LH ovulation predictor strips for greater accuracy.
- BBT (basal body temperature) confirms ovulation after the fact — useful for retrospective tracking.
- For irregular cycles, see a clinician — irregular ovulation can mask underlying conditions.
