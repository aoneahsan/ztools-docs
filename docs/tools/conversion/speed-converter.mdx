## Use cases

### Driving abroad

Speed limit signs in km/h while you're used to mph. Quick mental-check before being pulled over: 100 km/h = 62 mph, 80 km/h = 50 mph.

### Running and cycling pace

Convert "5 min/km" to mph or "8 mph" to a 7:30 mile pace. Useful when comparing training programs across countries.

### Sailing and aviation

Boat speeds are knots; converting to mph for landlubber friends. Aircraft speeds are knots and Mach; convert to km/h for airline schedules.

### Physics and engineering

Calculate kinetic energy: needs m/s. Convert from mph or km/h before plugging into KE = ½ m v².

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**What's a knot and why is it different from mph?**</summary>

1 knot = 1 nautical mile per hour = 1.852 km/h ≈ 1.151 mph. Used in maritime and aviation because it relates directly to angular distance on Earth (1 nm = 1 minute of latitude).

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I convert pace (min/km) to speed (km/h)?**</summary>

speed = 60 ÷ pace. So 5:00 min/km = 60 ÷ 5 = 12 km/h. The converter has a pace mode that handles this automatically.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What is Mach number?**</summary>

Speed as a fraction of the speed of sound at the same altitude/temperature. Mach 1 ≈ 343 m/s at sea level (changes with altitude). Used in aviation; "Mach 2" means twice the speed of sound.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How fast is the speed of light in everyday units?**</summary>

~300,000 km/s = ~186,000 mi/s = ~671 million mph. Practical for relativity calculations; meaningless for everyday vehicles.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Are speed and velocity the same?**</summary>

In physics: speed is the magnitude of velocity (no direction); velocity is a vector (with direction). The converter handles magnitudes only — direction is up to you to track.

</details>

## Tips

- For mph ↔ km/h: × 1.6 (kmh from mph), ÷ 1.6 (mph from kmh). Quick and approximate.
- For m/s from km/h: divide by 3.6 (e.g., 100 km/h = 27.78 m/s).
- Running pace is more intuitive than speed — most runners think in min/km or min/mi.
- For aviation, knots and Mach are universal; for cars, mph or km/h depending on country.
