# docx-to-pdf

A DOCX to PDF converter renders a Microsoft Word .docx document into a PDF file — preserving paragraph styles, fonts, images, tables, headers and footers, page numbering, and most layout features — producing a fixed-format PDF that displays identically on any device, regardless of installed fonts or Word version. The ZTools DOCX to PDF Converter runs entirely in your browser, supports multi-page documents, lets you choose page size (A4 default, US Letter, Legal), embeds standard fonts, and works on documents up to ~50 MB without server uploads.

## Use cases

### Sending a final document to clients

PDFs look identical on every device; .docx files render differently on Mac vs PC vs mobile. Convert before sending the final version of contracts, proposals, and reports.

### Submitting to forms that require PDF

Job applications, university submissions, government forms — most accept only PDF. Convert your .docx resume or cover letter before uploading.

### Archiving documents long-term

PDF is a stable, well-documented format for long-term archival. .docx specs change between Word versions; PDF is forward-compatible.

### Locking content from accidental edits

A .docx invites edits; a PDF discourages them. Convert when you want recipients to read, not modify.

## How it works

1. **Drop or select a .docx file** — File stays in your browser. Files up to ~50 MB work; larger files may slow the browser.
2. **Pick page size and margins** — A4 (default for international), US Letter (default for US), Legal, A3, custom. Margins: standard, narrow, wide, custom.
3. **Renderer parses the .docx** — The .docx is a zip containing XML; the renderer reads styles, paragraphs, tables, images, and lays them out per the document's formatting.
4. **Download the PDF** — Single file. Identical layout to what Word would print. Original .docx is unchanged.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Will the formatting be preserved exactly?**</summary>

Most formatting is preserved: fonts (when standard), paragraph styles, tables, images, headers/footers, page numbers. Edge cases that may differ: exotic fonts, complex SmartArt, embedded videos, ActiveX controls — these may render differently or not at all.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Does it preserve fonts?**</summary>

Standard fonts (Arial, Times New Roman, Calibri, etc.) are mapped to their PDF equivalents. Custom or licensed fonts may fall back to a similar substitute. For pixel-perfect rendering, embed the font in your .docx before converting.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is my document uploaded anywhere?**</summary>

No — conversion runs entirely in your browser. Important for confidential documents (contracts, NDAs, internal reports). Nothing leaves your device.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I convert PDF back to DOCX?**</summary>

Use the PDF to Word tool for the reverse direction. Note: DOCX → PDF is high-fidelity; PDF → DOCX often loses formatting because PDF doesn't store paragraph structure as cleanly.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Does it work for password-protected DOCX?**</summary>

No — the converter doesn't support encrypted .docx. Remove the password in Word first, then convert.

</details>

## Tips

- Always convert the final version, not drafts — converted PDFs are awkward to edit.
- For job applications, also save a clean .docx — some recruiters specifically want it.
- Embed unusual fonts in your .docx before converting if you need them to render correctly.
- For long documents, generate the PDF, then verify the first and last pages render correctly before sharing.
