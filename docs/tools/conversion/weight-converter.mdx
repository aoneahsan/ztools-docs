## Use cases

### Cooking and recipe scaling

British recipe says "8 oz of butter" — that's 227 g. Or scale a recipe by weight to half-portion: 500 g flour → 250 g.

### Fitness — body weight in different units

UK weight in stones (e.g., 12 st 6 lb), US in pounds (174 lb), gym tracker in kg (78.9 kg). Convert between them without confusion.

### Shipping and freight

Carrier charges by kg, but your scale reads pounds. Convert to enter the correct weight on the shipping form.

### Engineering and material specs

Steel beam datasheet uses kg/m; your project is in lb/ft. Convert linear density without dropping a digit.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Are mass and weight the same thing?**</summary>

In physics: no. Mass is the amount of matter; weight is the force gravity exerts on that mass. In everyday English, "weight" usually means mass — and that's how the converter treats it. For physics class, use Newtons (N) for true weight.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What's the difference between US and UK tons?**</summary>

US "short ton" = 2000 lb ≈ 907 kg. UK "long ton" = 2240 lb ≈ 1016 kg. Metric "tonne" = 1000 kg ≈ 2205 lb. Never assume a "ton" without context — always check.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I convert a person's weight from stones to pounds?**</summary>

1 stone = 14 pounds. So "12 st 6 lb" = 12 × 14 + 6 = 174 lb. The converter handles composite "X st Y lb" inputs automatically.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**For cooking, should I weigh by grams or ounces?**</summary>

Either works. Grams are more precise for small quantities (0.5 g resolution is common). Ounces work fine but the typical 1/8 oz resolution is coarser. Consistency within one recipe is more important than the unit choice.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Are the metric system and the SI system the same?**</summary>

SI is the modern, formally defined version of the metric system. Practically synonymous for length, mass, time. Older "metric" units (calories, dyne, gauss) aren't in SI but are still used in some fields.

</details>

## Tips

- Quick mental: 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb, 1 lb ≈ 0.45 kg, 1 oz ≈ 28 g.
- For body weight in stones: divide pounds by 14 (or multiply kg by 0.157).
- When shipping internationally, always specify the unit (kg or lb) — carriers default to different ones in different countries.
- For precision (medicine, chemistry), always use SI/metric — imperial fractions invite errors.
