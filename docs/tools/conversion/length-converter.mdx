## Use cases

### Shopping for clothes or furniture from international stores

A European chair lists "75 cm tall"; you think in inches. Convert to 29.5 in. Quick reality-check before ordering.

### Travel — running, hiking, driving distances

"The trail is 8 km" — that's 5 miles. "Speed limit 100 km/h" = 62 mph. Quick mental sanity-checks while abroad.

### Construction and DIY

Plans in metric; lumber in feet. Convert to plan dimensions you can actually buy. Avoid measuring twice and cutting wrong.

### Engineering specs

A part datasheet uses millimeters; your assembly drawing is in inches. Convert without dropping a decimal place — those errors propagate downstream.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**How exact are the conversions?**</summary>

Modern SI definitions make most conversions exact. 1 inch = 25.4 mm exactly. 1 mile = 1609.344 m exactly. The converter uses these exact factors with full floating-point precision.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What's a nautical mile and when is it used?**</summary>

1 nautical mile = 1852 m exactly. Defined as ~1 minute of latitude. Used in marine navigation, aviation, and meteorology where the spherical Earth makes statute miles awkward.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Why does the UK use both km and miles?**</summary>

Officially metric, but cars drive in mph and road signs are in miles for historical reasons. Cycling and running often use km; driving uses miles. UK is an unusual mix.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I convert "5'10"" to centimeters?**</summary>

5 feet = 152.4 cm, 10 inches = 25.4 cm, total = 177.8 cm. The converter parses "5'10"" automatically; you don't need to compute manually.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What about the astronomical unit (AU)?**</summary>

1 AU ≈ 149.6 million km — the Earth-Sun distance. Used in space science where km would produce inconveniently large numbers.

</details>

## Tips

- For quick mental conversion: 1 m ≈ 3.28 ft, 1 km ≈ 0.62 mi, 1 in = 2.54 cm.
- Always work in one unit system from start to finish — converting in the middle of a calculation is error-prone.
- For very large distances (astronomy), prefer light-years or parsecs; very small (microscopy) use micrometers or nanometers.
- Imperial measurements often have multiple decimal-point variants ("3 1/2 inches" vs "3.5 in") — convert to decimals before computing.
