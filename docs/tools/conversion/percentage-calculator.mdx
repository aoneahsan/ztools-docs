# percentage-calculator

A percentage calculator handles the four common percentage operations: (1) what is X% of Y (e.g. 15% of $80 = $12 tip), (2) X is what percent of Y (e.g. 35 out of 50 = 70%), (3) percentage change from X to Y (e.g. price up from $100 to $115 = +15%), (4) X% increase or decrease (e.g. $100 + 25% = $125). The ZTools Percentage Calculator covers all four with separate inputs, shows the formula explicitly for verification, supports negative changes (decreases), and has a tipping presets section for common dining/service tip rates.

## Use cases

### Tipping / service charges

Quick 15% / 18% / 20% tip on a restaurant bill. Calculator beats mental math when split among a group with tax.

### Discount / sale pricing

Product 30% off original $89.99 — final price? Calculator: $62.99. Avoid checkout-line surprises.

### Performance / KPI changes

Quarterly KPI moved from 50,000 to 56,500 — that's a 13% increase. Conversion ratio 3.2% → 3.8% = 18.75% relative increase. Calculator distinguishes absolute vs relative.

47 questions correct out of 60 = 78.3%. Quick grading aid.

### Tax calculation

Sales tax 7% on $245 → $17.15 tax + $262.15 total. VAT 20% on £100 ex-VAT → £120 inc-VAT.

3. **View result** — Result + formula shown explicitly. e.g. "15% of $80 = (15 / 100) × 80 = $12".

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Why does 100 + 25% then -25% not return to 100?**</summary>

100 × 1.25 = 125. 125 × 0.75 = 93.75. The percentages are computed against different bases (100 then 125). Common gotcha.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Compound interest vs simple interest — which here?**</summary>

Tool does single-period percentage math. For multi-period compound, use the financial-calculator tool — different formulas.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do percentage points differ from percentages?**</summary>

Conversion 3% → 4% is a "1 percentage-point increase" but a "33% relative increase". Different things; mixing them is the most common political/financial misstatement.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Tax-exclusive vs tax-inclusive — how to compute?**</summary>

$100 ex-VAT 20% = $120 inc-VAT. From $120 inc-VAT 20% back to ex-VAT: $120 / 1.2 = $100 (NOT $120 × 0.8 which is $96 — common error).

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Percentage of percentages?**</summary>

Always specify the base: 50% of 80% = 40% of original. Use this calculator twice: first 80% × something = X, then 50% of X.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Negative percentages?**</summary>

A change from 100 to 75 is -25%. The tool handles negative changes correctly with explicit sign in output.

</details>

## Tips

- For tipping, 20% of $X = $X / 5 — fastest mental shortcut.
- Tax-back-out: divide by (1 + tax%) as a decimal — e.g. $108 / 1.08 = $100 ex-tax (8% tax).
- Always specify "percentage" vs "percentage point" — they differ enormously.
- For investment returns, compounded over multiple years, use compound-interest calculator (NOT this percentage tool).
- For grading, percentage = correct / total × 100. 70% is the common minimum-pass threshold.
