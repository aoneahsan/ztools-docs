## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Why are there three "tons"?**</summary>

US short ton = 2000 lb = 907.185 kg. UK long ton = 2240 lb = 1016.05 kg. Metric tonne = 1000 kg = 2204.62 lb. They differ enough to matter in shipping and engineering — always specify which.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Stone — used where?**</summary>

Common in UK and Ireland for body weight (1 stone = 14 lb = 6.35 kg). Not used elsewhere. UK media reports human weight as "12 st 5 lb" rather than "173 lb" or "78 kg".

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Mass vs weight?**</summary>

Strictly: mass (kg) is the amount of matter; weight (newtons) is the force gravity exerts. Colloquially we say "weight" but mean mass. This converter handles mass; you wouldn't convert newtons to pounds (different physical quantity).

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is 1 oz of feathers the same as 1 oz of gold?**</summary>

Trick question — depends on the type of ounce. Avoirdupois ounce (28.35 g) is for everyday weight. Troy ounce (31.10 g) is for precious metals. So 1 troy oz of gold &gt; 1 avoir oz of feathers by ~10%. Tool clearly distinguishes.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How precise are conversions?**</summary>

1 lb = 453.59237 g exactly (since 1959 international yard and pound agreement). All other conversions are exact rational numbers; precision limited by float arithmetic.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Carat — same as karat?**</summary>

No: carat (200 mg) measures gemstone mass; karat (k or kt) measures gold purity (24k = pure). Different units for different things; same word origin.

</details>

## Tips

- Always specify which "ton" — US short, UK long, or metric tonne — they differ ~10–13%.
- For body weight, US uses lb, UK uses stone, rest of world uses kg.
- Cooking: metric (g) is more precise than imperial (oz, cups) — pro bakers prefer it.
- For gold/silver, troy ounce (31.1 g) is standard; for everything else, avoir ounce (28.35 g).
- 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb — useful mental math (off by 1% from exact 2.2046).
