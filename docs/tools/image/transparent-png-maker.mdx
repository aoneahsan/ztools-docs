# transparent-png-maker

A transparent PNG maker keys out a chosen colour (or near-colour range) from an image and replaces it with alpha transparency, producing a PNG you can drop onto any background — most useful for logos with white or solid-colour backdrops, screenshots from white pages, scanned line art, and product shots taken on a uniform sweep. The ZTools Transparent PNG Maker lets you click a pixel to set the key colour, adjust tolerance to expand or tighten the matched range, optionally feather the edge, and preview the alpha channel before exporting a clean PNG with no watermark.

## Use cases

### Logo cleanup for placement on dark backgrounds

A logo PDF/JPG comes with a white box around it. Click the white pixel, set tolerance ~20, export — instant transparent logo for use on coloured site headers.

### Screenshot UI mockups

Take a screenshot of a UI on a white page, key out the white, and the UI floats on any deck slide background.

### Scanned line art for digital colouring

Pencil/pen drawing scanned on a white sheet. Key out the white to get transparent linework you can colour beneath in any image editor.

### Product shots on white sweeps

Studio photo of a product on a white sweep. Key the white for an e-commerce-ready transparent cutout when AI background removal is overkill.

## How it works

1. **Upload image** — JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC. Drag-and-drop or paste.
2. **Click the colour to remove** — Eyedropper picks the exact RGB. Or type a hex value if you know the target colour.
3. **Set tolerance** — Slider 0–100. Low values match exact pixels; higher values include similar shades — useful for JPG compression artifacts around white.
4. **Optional feather** — Soften the alpha edge to avoid jagged keying on photographic content.
5. **Preview and export PNG** — Checker pattern shows transparency. Export as PNG (transparent), no watermark, original resolution.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Why does my logo have a white halo around the edge?**</summary>

JPG anti-aliasing blends the edge with the background, so even tolerance 30 leaves traces. Solutions: increase tolerance carefully, feather the edge, or re-export the logo from its vector source as a true transparent PNG.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**When should I use Background Remover instead?**</summary>

For photographs of people, products, or animals on cluttered backgrounds. The colour-key tool here is best for logos / screenshots / scans where the background is one solid colour.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I keep parts of the background and remove others?**</summary>

Yes — use the eraser/brush tools after keying to restore or remove specific regions.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Does it work on JPG?**</summary>

Input can be JPG; output is always PNG (JPG does not support transparency). If you save the JPG result, the transparency is lost.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Why is the output PNG so much bigger than the JPG input?**</summary>

PNG is lossless and stores alpha per pixel. For complex photographic transparency, expect 3–10x size growth vs JPG.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is the result watermarked?**</summary>

No. Output PNG is clean, fully owned by you, free for commercial use.

</details>

## Tips

- Pick the worst-offending background pixel — the keying expands from that exact RGB.
- When fighting JPG compression halos, feather 1–2 px is usually enough; more makes logos look mushy.
- For multi-colour backgrounds, key the dominant colour first, then run a second pass on the remainder.
- Always re-export logos from their vector source if you have it — it gives the cleanest transparent PNG.
- After keying, run the result through the Image Compressor to keep PNG size under control.
