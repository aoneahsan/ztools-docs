# video-thumbnail-generator

A video thumbnail generator extracts one or more frames from a video at chosen timestamps and saves them as JPG or PNG images, used for video previews, social-media post thumbnails, blog post hero images, video-product hero shots, and visual chapter markers. The ZTools Video Thumbnail Generator runs entirely in the browser using HTML5 video + Canvas, supports MP4 / WebM / MOV / OGG inputs, lets you scrub to any timestamp and capture, and exports images at the video's native resolution — without uploading the source video.

## Use cases

### YouTube / video-platform thumbnail

Pick a single representative frame from your uploaded video to serve as the click-through thumbnail. Better thumbnails have measurably higher click-through rates.

### Blog hero image from screencast

For a tutorial blog post that includes a screencast video, extract a key frame as the hero image. Visual continuity between hero and embedded video.

### Video chapter markers

Long-form video split into chapters; one thumbnail per chapter for an interactive table of contents. Extract at chapter timestamps; assemble into a contents page.

### Static product image from video

E-commerce product video; pull a clean still as the static catalog image. Avoids needing a separate photo shoot.

## How it works

1. **Upload video** — Drag-drop or pick. Browser loads via HTML5 &lt;video&gt;; immediate preview.
2. **Scrub to timestamp** — Drag the playhead to the desired frame. Frame-by-frame stepping (←/→ keys where supported) for precise selection.
3. **Capture frame** — Tool draws current frame to a Canvas at video's native resolution; encodes as JPG (smaller) or PNG (lossless).
4. **Repeat for multiple frames** — Capture multiple thumbnails at different timestamps; download all as a ZIP.
5. **Download** — JPG or PNG saves locally. Native resolution preserved unless you opt to resize.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**What format should I pick?**</summary>

JPG: smaller, lossy; great for photographic content. PNG: larger, lossless; preserves crisp UI / screenshot details. Pick based on the frame's content.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I get a sharp frame?**</summary>

Pause exactly on a still moment (not mid-motion). Motion blur in the source video transfers to the thumbnail. Pause on cleared frames.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Why does the thumbnail look pixellated?**</summary>

Source video resolution determines maximum thumbnail size. Cannot make a 720p video produce a clean 4K thumbnail. Match thumbnail target to source resolution.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is the video uploaded?**</summary>

No — HTML5 video and Canvas process entirely in the browser. Source video never leaves your device.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I overlay text on the thumbnail?**</summary>

Not in this tool. Generate the still here, then add text in an image editor (Photoshop, Figma, Pixlr, etc.).

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What about animated thumbnails (GIF)?**</summary>

Use the video-to-gif tool instead. GIFs autoplay in many platforms; great for hover previews.

</details>

## Tips

- For best click-through rates, pick frames showing faces, action, or strong contrast. A static dim frame underperforms.
- Avoid the very first frame of a video — usually a still title card or fade-in. Frame at 1-3 seconds in tends to be the most representative.
- For YouTube specifically, custom thumbnails with text overlay outperform raw frames significantly. Use the extracted frame as a base, add text in an editor.
- Save in PNG for thumbnails you may edit later; JPG only when going straight to publish.
- Capture multiple candidates per video; pick the best after seeing all options side-by-side.
