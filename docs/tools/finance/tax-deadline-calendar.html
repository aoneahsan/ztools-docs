Tax Deadline Calendar

A tax deadline calendar is a reference tool that lists the standard 2025–2026 US federal individual tax dates in one place and lets you download them as an .ics file you can import into any calendar app. It marks the milestones that matter for most filers: the four quarterly estimated-tax due dates, the April filing-or-extension deadline, the last day for prior-year IRA and HSA contributions, and the October extended-return deadline. It performs no calculations — it simply shows the dates and generates reminders. Deadlines can shift to the next business day, disaster relief may extend them, and state dates differ, so always confirm with the IRS.

Use cases​

Never miss a quarterly estimated-tax date​

Self-employed people, freelancers, and investors who owe estimated tax must pay four times a year, and a missed payment can trigger an underpayment penalty. Importing the calendar drops the January 15, April 15, June 15, and September 15 due dates straight into your phone with reminders, so the deadlines come to you instead of you trying to remember them.

Plan the April filing crunch​

April 15, 2026 carries several deadlines at once — filing your 2025 return or requesting an extension, paying any tax owed, making 2025 IRA and HSA contributions, and the first 2026 estimated payment. Seeing them grouped on one date helps you plan the weeks before so nothing slips through in the last-minute rush. An extension gives you until October to file, but not to pay, which the calendar’s notes make clear.

Remember the IRA and HSA contribution cutoff​

The deadline to put money into a prior-year IRA or HSA is the April filing date, not December 31, and many savers miss the extra window. A reminder on April 15, 2026 for 2025 contributions gives you a last chance to top up retirement or health savings and potentially lower your tax before the door closes.

Track an extension to October​

If you filed for an extension, your 2025 return is due October 15, 2026 — but the extension only delays filing, not payment. Keeping the October date on your calendar, alongside the summer Q2 and Q3 estimated dates, prevents the extended deadline from sneaking up after tax season fades from mind. Adding a personal buffer a week earlier gives you room to gather documents.

Give a household or small business one shared schedule​

A shared tax calendar keeps a couple, a household, or a small business on the same page about who owes what and when. Because the file imports into Google, Apple, or Outlook calendars, everyone involved sees the same estimated-tax and filing reminders, reducing the odds that a payment is duplicated or forgotten between people.

How it works​

  1. Review the listed deadlines — The page shows each standard 2025–2026 federal individual deadline with its date and a short note on what is due.
  2. Read the notes on each date — Notes flag shared deadlines like April 15 and clarify that an extension delays filing but not payment.
  3. Download the .ics file — Click to generate a standard iCalendar file containing every listed deadline as an event with a reminder.
  4. Import it into your calendar app — Open the .ics in Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or Outlook so the dates and alerts appear alongside your own schedule.
  5. Confirm and adjust for your situation — Verify each date against the IRS, since weekends, holidays, disaster relief, and state rules can move the deadlines the calendar shows.

Frequently asked questions​

Is this a tax calculator?

No. It does no math and computes no amounts. It is a reference calendar of standard federal deadlines plus a downloadable .ics file to help you plan and set reminders.

What are the main 2025–2026 deadlines?

January 15, 2026 (Q4 2025 estimated tax), April 15, 2026 (file or extend, pay, IRA/HSA contributions, Q1 estimated), June 15, 2026 (Q2), September 15, 2026 (Q3), October 15, 2026 (extended return), and January 15, 2027 (Q4 2026 estimated).

What happens if a deadline is a weekend or holiday?

The IRS moves the due date to the next business day. The calendar shows the standard date, so verify the exact business-day deadline with the IRS for any given year.

Does an extension give me more time to pay?

No. An extension moves the filing deadline to October 15 but not the payment deadline. Tax owed is still due by April 15, or interest and penalties can accrue.

Are state tax deadlines included?

No. This covers federal individual deadlines only. State income-tax deadlines often differ, and some states have no income tax, so check your state’s tax agency separately.

Why might my real deadline differ from the calendar?

Weekends and holidays shift dates to the next business day, federally declared disasters can postpone them, and special situations (like living abroad) have their own rules. Always confirm with the IRS.

Is anything stored when I download the file?

No. The .ics is generated in your browser and nothing is uploaded. What you do with the file afterward is entirely on your own device and calendar app.

Tips​

  • Set the reminders a week or two early so you have time to gather documents and make payments.
  • Treat the dates as standard references and confirm the exact business-day deadline with the IRS each year.
  • Add your state’s deadlines separately — this calendar covers federal individual dates only.
  • Note that April 15 stacks several deadlines; plan the weeks before rather than the day itself.
  • If you live in a disaster area or abroad, check the IRS for extended dates that may apply to you.
