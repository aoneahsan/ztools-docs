# Palladium Rate Calculator

A palladium rate calculator computes the metal value of palladium items — used in jewelry (especially white-gold alloys), industrial applications (catalytic converters dominate global demand), and investment bars — by combining weight, purity, and spot price. The ZTools Palladium Rate Calculator runs entirely in the browser, supports the common purity standards (.950 jewelry, .500 alloyed, .9995 investment bars), gram / ounce / kilogram inputs, and is useful for jewelry valuation, catalytic-converter scrap negotiation, and investment-bar verification.

## Use cases

### Catalytic-converter recycling

Modern petrol catalytic converters contain palladium. Recyclers price by extractable metal weight; the calculator estimates the floor value.

### Palladium jewelry sale

Some white-gold alternatives are .950 palladium. Calculate melt to gauge offers.

### Investment bar verification

Confirm the spot-implied price of a 1 oz palladium bar before completing a deal.

### Portfolio diversification context

Palladium often moves independently of gold and silver — knowing per-gram values helps right-size positions.

## How it works

1. **Enter spot palladium price** — Per gram or per troy ounce. Palladium has been highly volatile in the past decade; confirm rate before transacting.
2. **Pick weight unit** — Grams, ounces, kilograms.
3. **Enter purity** — .950 (jewelry), .500 (alloy), .9995 (investment).
4. **Compute melt value** — weight × purity × spot.
5. **Cross-check against gold/platinum** — Optional comparison view; palladium trades differently from both.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Why is palladium so volatile?**</summary>

The market is small and dominated by Russian / South African supply. Auto-industry demand (gasoline catalytic converters) creates large swings when policy or production changes.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can palladium be substituted for platinum?**</summary>

In some catalysts and jewelry alloys, yes. The substitution flow has driven big price moves over the past decade.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How does palladium jewelry compare to platinum?**</summary>

Cheaper per gram, lighter (less dense at 12.0 g/cc vs platinum 21.45 g/cc), similarly hypoallergenic.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Are palladium bars liquid?**</summary>

Less liquid than gold or silver bars. Reputable refiner brands (PAMP, Credit Suisse, Royal Canadian Mint) trade more actively.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Should I cash in a catalytic converter from an old car?**</summary>

It is often worth $50–500 depending on the vehicle and metal content. Theft of catalytic converters is a real problem because of this; sell legally with vehicle title proof.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is rhodium also in my converter?**</summary>

Yes — modern converters contain platinum + palladium + rhodium. Rhodium has been worth more per gram than the others at times. Recyclers price the bundle.

</details>

## Tips

- Confirm rate at the moment of transaction — palladium can swing 5%+ in a day.
- For converter scrap, get multiple quotes — recycler margins vary widely.
- Investment bars from recognised refiners hold value best; off-brand bars trade at larger discounts.
- Palladium jewelry resale market is thin — expect deep discounts to spot when selling.
- Diversification rule of thumb: hold no more in any one PGM (platinum group metal) than you can lose without panic.
