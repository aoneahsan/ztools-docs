# Paycheck Calculator

A paycheck calculator estimates take-home pay (net pay) from gross wages by subtracting federal / state income tax, payroll taxes (Social Security + Medicare in the US, National Insurance in the UK, comparable in other countries), and pre-tax deductions (401(k), health-insurance premium, HSA / FSA contributions). The ZTools Paycheck Calculator runs in the browser, supports both salaried and hourly inputs, lets you toggle pay frequency (weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, monthly), and is **an educational estimator only — actual pay depends on local tax rules, filing status, dependants, and many edge cases; verify any number with your employer's payroll provider before making financial decisions**.

## Use cases

### Job-offer evaluation

A $90K offer in California vs $85K in Texas — federal tax is similar, but California state tax + cost-of-living shifts the real take-home meaningfully.

### Side-gig take-home check

A consultant invoices $5K/month — what lands in the bank after self-employment tax and quarterly estimates? Calculator approximates.

### 401(k) contribution planning

Increasing 401(k) from 5% to 10% — what is the actual take-home reduction? Often less than expected due to tax savings.

### Hourly-to-annual translation

$25/hour at 40 hours/week — net annual after taxes? Calculator shows both before and after deductions.

## How it works

1. **Pick pay type and frequency** — Salaried (annual amount) or hourly (rate + hours). Pay frequency: weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, monthly.
2. **Enter gross pay per period** — For salaried, the calculator divides annual into per-period.
3. **Enter pre-tax deductions** — 401(k) %, health-insurance premium, HSA/FSA, commuter benefits — anything pre-tax.
4. **Apply taxes** — Federal tax via brackets, state tax via flat % or local schedule, FICA / NI / equivalent payroll tax.
5. **Show net pay** — Gross − pre-tax deductions − taxes = take-home. Annualised view also shown.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Why is my actual paycheck different from the calculator?**</summary>

Many reasons: pre-tax deductions you did not enter (insurance, HSA), additional withholdings (e.g., supplemental income), state-specific items, post-tax deductions (Roth 401(k)), bonuses. Treat as a ballpark.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How is FICA calculated?**</summary>

US Social Security: 6.2% on wages up to the annual wage base. Medicare: 1.45% on all wages, plus 0.9% additional Medicare tax above thresholds. Total: 7.65% for most workers, employer matches.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Does pre-tax deduction reduce my take-home dollar-for-dollar?**</summary>

No — it reduces taxable income too. So $100 pre-tax 401(k) reduces take-home by ~$70–80 depending on bracket. Effective "cost" of saving is less than the amount saved.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Should I use Roth or traditional 401(k)?**</summary>

Depends on your tax bracket now vs in retirement. The calculator can model traditional (pre-tax) easily; Roth (post-tax) reduces take-home but produces tax-free retirement withdrawals.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Are bonuses taxed differently?**</summary>

In the US, supplemental wages are withheld at flat 22% for the first $1M. Actual tax owed at year-end matches your normal bracket — overwithholding is refunded.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Will it tell me if my employer is withholding correctly?**</summary>

It is an estimate. For real verification, check your year-end pay stubs vs filed taxes; significant mismatches warrant a payroll inquiry.

</details>

## Tips

- Treat the result as a ballpark — actual paycheck has many fine-grained inputs.
- For job-offer comparisons, use the same calculator for both offers — apples to apples.
- Pre-tax retirement savings cost less than they save — the tax break makes contributions more affordable than they look.
- Verify state / local tax rates for your specific jurisdiction.
- After a major tax-law change, recompute — withholding tables shift annually.
