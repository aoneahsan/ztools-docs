# Loan Calculator

A loan calculator computes the monthly payment, total interest paid over the term, and total cost (principal + interest) for any standard amortising loan — mortgages, auto loans, personal loans, student loans — given principal, annual rate, and term. The ZTools Loan Calculator runs in the browser, supports side-by-side comparison of two or three offers, generates the full amortisation schedule, models extra payments to show interest savings, and surfaces the total-cost-of-credit perspective that monthly-payment thinking alone hides.

## Use cases

### Comparing two mortgage offers

20-year vs 30-year fixed at slightly different rates. Both monthly payments and total interest matter; calculator shows both.

### Sizing a personal loan

Based on a max monthly payment you can afford, work backward to the maximum principal that fits your budget at quoted rate / term.

### Refinancing analysis

Existing loan at 7% vs refinance offer at 5% with $2,000 closing costs. Calculator computes break-even point in months.

### Auto loan term tradeoff

60-month vs 72-month — longer term lowers payment but increases total interest. See the tradeoff numerically.

## How it works

1. **Enter loan parameters** — Principal, annual rate (APR), term (months or years).
2. **Compute payment** — Standard amortisation: P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1) where r is monthly rate.
3. **Show totals** — Monthly payment, total interest, total cost.
4. **Compare offers** — Side-by-side panel: monthly payment, total interest, total cost, break-even point if there are upfront costs.
5. **Model extra payments** — Add a recurring or one-time prepayment; see new term and interest saved.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**What is APR vs interest rate?**</summary>

Interest rate is the cost of borrowing only. APR includes lender fees and closing costs spread over the term. APR is the more honest comparison metric for loan offers.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is monthly payment the right comparison?**</summary>

No — total cost over the loan is what matters. A lower payment over a much longer term often costs vastly more. Always look at total interest.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I model variable rates?**</summary>

Default is fixed rate. For variable, run the calculator at the initial rate; once the rate adjusts, recompute with the then-outstanding balance and remaining term.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Are closing costs included?**</summary>

Not by default. Add them to principal if you want a worst-case "total" or compare offers separately.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How are payments allocated to principal vs interest?**</summary>

Each month: interest = balance × monthly rate. Principal = payment − interest. New balance = old balance − principal. Early-life payments are mostly interest; that flips later.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Should I always take the longest term for the lowest payment?**</summary>

No — longer terms cost dramatically more total interest. Pick the shortest term you can afford comfortably; aggressive principal reduction beats payment minimisation.

</details>

## Tips

- Compare total interest, not monthly payments.
- Run the prepayment scenario — a small monthly extra often saves shocking amounts of total interest.
- For mortgages, consider a 15-year vs 30-year — the rate is usually lower and the total cost dramatically less.
- Always include closing costs / fees in the comparison — APR captures these.
- Refinance break-even: divide closing costs by monthly savings — that is months to break even.
