# jewelry-metal-calculator

A jewelry metal calculator estimates the combined metal value of a multi-piece jewelry collection — handling pieces in gold, silver, platinum, and palladium across different purities — by computing each piece's melt value and summing them, useful when valuing an estate, preparing for a bulk sale, organising an inventory for insurance, or just understanding the metal worth of a household's accumulated jewelry. The ZTools Jewelry Metal Calculator runs entirely in the browser, supports gram / ounce / tola entries, lets you save the inventory locally, and exports the results as CSV for record-keeping or appraiser hand-off.

## Use cases

### Estate inventory

After a relative's death, weigh and catalogue every metal piece. Calculator gives a metal-only baseline to share with the executor and any appraiser.

### Pre-sale evaluation

Before approaching a gold buyer with a bulk lot, know the total melt value across all pieces. Buyers cannot lowball when you have the math.

### Insurance schedule preparation

For renters / homeowners insurance, schedule jewelry above the standard limit. Metal value plus markup gives a defensible per-piece number for plain pieces; gemstones still need separate appraisal.

### Personal record-keeping

A simple CSV per family member of "what we own and roughly what it is worth" reduces stress when life events force liquidations.

## How it works

1. **Add pieces one at a time** — For each: name, metal type, purity, weight, optional notes (gemstones, hallmarks).
2. **Set spot prices per metal** — Gold, silver, platinum, palladium — your local-currency rate. Update before computing.
3. **Compute per-piece melt** — weight × purity × spot price for each piece.
4. **Sum the collection** — Totals by metal and grand total.
5. **Save and export** — Saves locally in your browser; CSV export for spreadsheets and record-keeping.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Does it factor making charges?**</summary>

No — it is melt-value only. Making charges, antique premiums, and gemstones are separate. Use the calculator as a floor; engage an appraiser for total fair-market value.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I weigh a piece with a gemstone?**</summary>

Two approaches: (1) take to a jeweller who unmounts the stone for a clean weight, or (2) use published average weights for the setting style. Approach 1 is more accurate for valuable pieces.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I save the inventory across devices?**</summary>

Local-only by default. Optional CSV export lets you store in cloud drives if you want it accessible elsewhere.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Should I share this with my insurance?**</summary>

It is a starting point. Insurance typically wants a formal appraisal for scheduled items above the standard policy limit. The CSV output gives the appraiser a head start.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How accurate are stamped purities?**</summary>

Generally reliable from reputable jewellers, but counterfeit hallmarks exist. For high-value pieces, an independent acid test or XRF confirms.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Does it handle non-precious metals (stainless, brass, bronze)?**</summary>

No — these have minimal scrap value and are not handled. The calculator focuses on the four precious metals where the math meaningfully matters.

</details>

## Tips

- Photograph each piece alongside the weighing scale reading — proof of inventory.
- Update spot prices before any actual transaction — values shift weekly.
- For insurance, complete an inventory once per year; lock the CSV in cloud backup.
- For sales, sort pieces by purity and pitch as lots ("all 22K, total X grams") — buyers pay better than for mixed mystery batches.
- Treat melt value as the floor; antiques and craftsmanship can multiply it.
