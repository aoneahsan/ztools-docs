# Compress PDF Advanced

A PDF compressor reduces a PDF's file size, mostly by re-encoding the images inside it. The ZTools Compress PDF tool has two modes. On our server, it re-encodes the photos and scans inside the PDF as JPEG and scales down oversized ones at a light, balanced or strong setting, while text, links and vector graphics stay as they are, so the text remains selectable and searchable; it takes PDFs up to 50 MB and 500 pages, needs a free Google sign-in, and deletes your file right after processing. On this device, nothing is uploaded: a lossless re-save gives modest savings with everything untouched, and a rasterise option turns each page into an image at the resolution you choose, which shrinks scanned PDFs a lot but makes the text unselectable.

## Use cases

### Squeezing a 25 MB report under a 10 MB email attachment limit

Outlook, Gmail, and most corporate mail servers cap attachments around 10-25 MB. Scanned reports usually shrink a lot, because they are mostly image data. Saves the awkward "I had to use WeTransfer" follow-up.

### Preparing PDFs for download from a website

A 5 MB whitepaper is slow to open on a mobile connection. Compressing its images makes the download noticeably quicker.

### Archiving PDF invoices or contracts cheaply

Scanned invoices and contracts take far less storage once their images are compressed, and with the server mode their text layer stays searchable.

### Uploading PDFs to forms with strict size limits

Government portals, school applications, and visa systems often cap uploads at 1-2 MB per file. Compress to fit and submit on the first try.

## How it works

1. **Add your PDF** — Any PDF that is not password-protected. The server mode takes up to 50 MB and 500 pages.
2. **Choose where to compress** — "On our server" keeps text selectable and needs a free Google sign-in. "On this device" works in your browser with no upload.
3. **Pick the strength** — On our server: light, balanced or strong. On this device: a lossless re-save, or rasterising at a chosen resolution and image quality.
4. **Compress** — Photos and scans are re-encoded; text becomes an image only if you pick the on-device rasterise option.
5. **Download and verify** — Save the compressed PDF; open in your preferred reader to confirm legibility before sharing or archiving.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**How small can a PDF get?**</summary>

Image-heavy PDFs (scans, brochures) often shrink a lot. Text-heavy PDFs shrink little because text is already compact. The actual ratio depends on what's inside the file.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Will compression hurt my PDF's quality?**</summary>

It depends on the strength. Strong is fine for on-screen reading but may look soft when printed; light keeps near-original sharpness. Always check the result before sharing.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Will my PDF be uploaded?**</summary>

Only in the "On our server" mode, which sends it over HTTPS to our own processing server; the server deletes the PDF and the result right after processing. The "On this device" mode never uploads anything.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Does the tool work on text-only PDFs?**</summary>

Yes, but savings are modest because text is already compactly stored. The biggest savings come from PDFs containing embedded images, especially high-resolution scans.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is OCR (text extraction from scans) preserved?**</summary>

On our server, yes — only the images are re-encoded, so an existing text layer stays searchable. The on-device rasterise option turns pages into images and removes it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I compress an encrypted/password-protected PDF?**</summary>

Unlock it first using our PDF Unlock tool (you must know the password). Compressing an encrypted PDF directly will fail.

</details>

## Tips

- Try balanced first — it is the best trade-off for most PDFs.
- For archival storage, strong is fine when the file only needs to be read on screen.
- For documents that will be printed, stay on light.
- Combine with PDF Splitter when each part needs to fit a separate size limit.
