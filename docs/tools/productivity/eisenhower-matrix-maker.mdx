# eisenhower-matrix-maker

An Eisenhower Matrix Maker is a 2×2 priority-grid tool, attributed to President Dwight Eisenhower, that classifies every task by two dimensions — urgent vs not urgent, important vs not important — landing each into one of four quadrants with a clear instruction: Do (urgent + important), Schedule (important + not urgent), Delegate (urgent + not important), Drop (neither). The ZTools Eisenhower Matrix Maker runs entirely in the browser, supports drag-and-drop between quadrants, persistence across sessions, optional task notes / due dates, and a print / share view for team alignment.

## Use cases

### Weekly task triage

Sunday evening: dump everything on your mind into the inbox; sort into quadrants. Monday: focus on Q1 (Do) + Q2 (Schedule). Q3 (Delegate) and Q4 (Drop) shrink the apparent workload immediately.

### Overwhelmed-feeling reset

When everything feels equally urgent, the matrix exposes the lie. Most "urgent" items are not important; sorting them into Q3 (Delegate) or Q4 (Drop) recovers mental space.

### Manager prioritisation with team

Run the matrix on team backlog. Decide together what is Do, Schedule, Delegate, Drop. Surfaces hidden disagreement on what matters; aligns team.

### Personal life-and-work

Mix work + personal tasks into the matrix. The matrix doesn't care about category — only urgency × importance. Often surfaces personal tasks (health, relationships) as Q2 that work was crowding out.

## How it works

1. **List all tasks** — Inbox area: dump everything you might need to do without filtering yet. Comprehensive capture is step one.
2. **Drag each into a quadrant** — Q1 Urgent + Important = Do. Q2 Important + Not urgent = Schedule. Q3 Urgent + Not important = Delegate. Q4 Neither = Drop.
3. **Action per quadrant** — Q1: do today. Q2: time-block in calendar this week. Q3: hand to someone or automate. Q4: delete; do not feel guilty.
4. **Re-sort weekly** — Tasks migrate over time. A Q2 ignored long enough becomes Q1. Weekly re-sort prevents that drift.
5. **Track Q2 share** — Healthy work is mostly Q2. Lots of Q1 = chronic firefighting; lots of Q3 = misaligned demands; lots of Q4 = noise problem.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**How do I distinguish urgent from important?**</summary>

Urgent = has a near-term deadline; important = aligned with goals. They are independent. A meeting at 3pm is urgent; not necessarily important. Studying for an exam in 6 weeks is important; not yet urgent.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What if everything feels important?**</summary>

It usually isn't. Force-rank: which 3 tasks, if completed, would make this week a success? Those are important. The rest, less so.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**I can't delegate at my level — what do I do with Q3?**</summary>

Q3 without a delegate option becomes "automate / decline / batch". Many Q3 items are interruptions that can be batched (email at fixed times) or declined politely.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is Q4 just procrastination dressed up?**</summary>

No. Q4 contains genuine noise: notifications, low-value reading, busywork. Procrastination is avoiding Q1 / Q2 by doing Q3 / Q4 — different problem.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How often should I re-sort?**</summary>

Weekly minimum. Daily is usually overkill; quarterly is too coarse. Sunday evening or Monday morning sorting works for most.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Where is my data?**</summary>

Local browser storage only. Export JSON for backup. No server upload, no account.

</details>

## Tips

- Comprehensive capture first — sorting is meaningless if you only sort 50% of your real tasks.
- Schedule Q2 time-blocks the moment you sort. Without scheduling, Q2 stays "later" forever and converts to Q1.
- Delegate aggressively. Q3 done by you is wasted time; Q3 done by someone whose role it actually is is good time.
- Track Q4 items briefly (1 week) before archiving — most people are surprised at how many Q4 items they had been mentally tracking.
- For team use, run the matrix together. Disagreement on quadrants surfaces priority misalignment.
