# goal-tracker

A goal tracker is a longer-horizon planning tool that captures multi-month or multi-year goals, breaks each into measurable milestones, records weekly check-in progress, and surfaces percentage completion — closing the gap between annual ambition and weekly behaviour where most goals quietly die. The ZTools Goal Tracker runs entirely in the browser, supports SMART-style goal definition (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound), milestone breakdown, weekly check-in journaling, progress percentage, and persists across sessions in browser storage.

## Use cases

### Annual personal goals

New year goals (read 24 books, lose 15 pounds, save $10k). Tracker breaks each into monthly milestones, weekly check-ins keep them visible. Without tracking, ~92% of new-year goals fail.

### Quarterly OKRs (work)

Objectives and Key Results format: 1 objective + 3-5 key results with measurable targets. Tracker maintains the 0-100% scorecard expected by management; weekly check-ins prevent end-of-quarter surprises.

### Career development plan

Multi-year professional goals (promotion, certification, side income). Quarterly milestones; weekly check-ins for habit-level progress. Promotes patience over impulse.

### Fitness or training programs

Marathon training (20 weeks), strength program (12 weeks), language acquisition (6 months). Milestones = specific check-ins (e.g. complete 16-mile run, lift 1.5×bodyweight). Trackable, motivating.

## How it works

1. **Define each goal** — SMART format: specific outcome, measurable target, by-when date. "Read 24 books in 2026" beats "read more".
2. **Break into milestones** — 4-12 milestones per goal. Each has its own due date. Reaching milestones provides motivation between final completion and start.
3. **Weekly check-in** — 5 min/week per goal: progress % update, what worked, what blocked. Compounds into much better self-awareness over months.
4. **Progress visualisation** — Tracker shows current % vs expected % at this point in the year/quarter. Surfaces ahead/on/behind status visually.
5. **End-of-period review** — Quarterly / annually: reflect on completion, lessons, next-period goals. Closes the loop.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Why do most goals fail?**</summary>

They are vague, lack milestones, and have no review cadence. Tracking forces specificity. Specificity + visibility + weekly review beats annual willpower.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How many goals can I track at once?**</summary>

3-5 across personal + work. More than 7 dilutes focus; "everything is a priority" means none are. Pick the most important; let others wait until next quarter.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What is SMART?**</summary>

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. "Read 24 books by Dec 31" is SMART; "read more" is not. The framework forces the goal into testable form.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How is goal tracking different from habit tracking?**</summary>

Habits are recurring behaviours (exercise daily). Goals are time-bound outcomes (run a marathon by June). Habits often serve goals — daily training habit serves marathon goal.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What if I miss a milestone?**</summary>

Adjust the plan or accept the slip. Hide-and-pretend kills goals; honest re-baseline preserves them. Sometimes the right answer is a smaller goal.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I avoid demotivation when behind?**</summary>

Re-ground in the why. The metric is a proxy; the underlying motivation drives. If the why has weakened, consider whether the goal still matters.

</details>

## Tips

- Write the goal in active language with a deadline: "By Q4 2026, I will have …".
- Break into max-3-month milestones. Longer than 3 months without a check-in invites drift.
- Schedule weekly check-ins on the calendar — not optional. 5 minutes; same time every week.
- Track a maximum of 5 goals — focus is the multiplier.
- Quarterly review beats end-of-year. Adjust early, often, and honestly.
