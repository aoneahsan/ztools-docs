# habit-tracker

A habit tracker is a daily logging tool where each habit (exercise, reading, meditation, study) is checked off when completed and visualised across days as a streak or grid, leveraging the well-documented effect of don't-break-the-chain motivation to sustain behaviour change. The ZTools Habit Tracker runs entirely in the browser, supports unlimited habits, streak counters, monthly grid view, weekly + monthly completion percentages, and persists data in browser storage — no sign-up, no cloud sync, your habits stay private.

## Use cases

### Building a daily routine

Track 4-6 daily habits (exercise, water intake, reading, sleep schedule). Visual streak across weeks builds momentum; missing one day is acceptable, missing two breaks the pattern.

### Recovery from breaking a streak

After a missed day, the tracker shows the gap. Studies (Phillippa Lally, 2010) show 1 missed day rarely derails habit formation; 2-3 in a row often does. Visualising the gap motivates the next-day restart.

### Weekly review

Sunday: glance at the week — which habits sustained, which slipped. Identify pattern (e.g. "exercise drops on Thursdays — why?") and adjust environment, not willpower.

### Family / parent-child accountability

Children practising piano, reading, or chores can track their own. The visual feedback loop is more motivating than parental nagging.

## How it works

1. **Add habits** — List the habits you want to build. 3-6 per tracker is realistic; more than that and adherence drops.
2. **Mark each day** — End of day (or as you complete), tap each habit to mark done. Today and yesterday are easiest; gaps over 2 days require manual back-fill.
3. **Track streaks** — Tracker counts consecutive days completed for each habit. Longest streak + current streak both shown.
4. **Calendar grid view** — Month-at-a-glance: each row = habit, each column = day, filled cells = done. Patterns (weekday vs weekend) become obvious.
5. **Weekly summary** — Completion % per habit per week. 80% completion is healthy long-term; aim for 100% only on critical habits.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**How long does it take to form a habit?**</summary>

Phillippa Lally (UCL, 2010): median 66 days for behaviour to feel automatic. Range 18-254 days. Simpler habits (drink water) form faster than complex ones (write 1000 words).

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What if I miss a day?**</summary>

One miss rarely matters. The same study showed missing one day did not measurably affect long-term habit formation. Two in a row is the warning; three is the trend reversal.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How many habits can I track simultaneously?**</summary>

3-6 in active formation. Tracking 10+ rarely works — attention dilutes. Once a habit is automatic (after 60+ days), drop it from active tracking.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is this just a to-do list?**</summary>

No — to-do lists track unique tasks. Habit trackers measure recurring behaviour over time. The pattern visualisation is the point.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Where is my data?**</summary>

Local storage only. No server, no account. Export JSON for backup. Privacy by design.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Why visual streaks instead of just numbers?**</summary>

Loss aversion. A long visible streak triggers "do not break it" motivation that pure completion percentages do not. Jerry Seinfeld popularised the don't-break-the-chain method for joke writing.

</details>

## Tips

- Start with 1-3 habits, not 10. Master the rhythm before scaling.
- Stack habits onto existing cues — "after morning coffee" → meditate. Easier than building from scratch.
- Track at the end of the day, not the start — start-of-day intent ≠ end-of-day completion.
- Forgive missed days but not multiple in a row. The 2-day rule prevents drift.
- Review weekly, adjust monthly. Habits that consistently miss reflect environment, not effort — change the environment.
