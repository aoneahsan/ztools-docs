# Decision Wheel Spinner

A decision wheel spinner is an animated random-choice tool where you list options as wheel segments and click to spin, landing on one at random — a fast, visually-engaging alternative to coin flips, dice, or "let's vote" deadlocks. The ZTools Decision Wheel Spinner runs entirely in the browser, uses cryptographically-strong randomness for fair outcomes, supports unlimited segments, custom colours, weighted (uneven) segments, sound-on-spin, and works on any device including projection for classroom or team use.

## Use cases

### Stuck-in-decision pairs

Couple deciding dinner; family choosing weekend activity. The wheel breaks deadlock without anyone "winning" the argument. Outcome rarely matters; the deadlock cost was the bigger problem.

### Classroom random picker

Teacher needs to call on a student. Wheel of names ensures fairness (no bias toward extroverts). Students often experience this as fun rather than threatening.

### Team standup or icebreaker

Random ordering for who speaks first; random pairing for breakout rooms; random topic for icebreaker. Lower-cognitive-load fun.

### Raffle / giveaway draw

List participants on segments, spin, the landed segment wins. Visible randomness builds trust in the outcome.

## How it works

1. **Add segments** — One per option. Segments auto-size to fill the wheel evenly; can be weighted (one option larger = higher chance) for asymmetric draws.
2. **Customise** — Colour per segment, optional emoji or icon, sound on spin start / stop.
3. **Click spin** — Wheel rotates with deceleration. Spin angle determined by Crypto-API random (32-bit) — no shortcut to guess outcome.
4. **Read result** — Pointer at top indicates winning segment. Result displayed below; option to remove that segment for "draw without replacement".
5. **Save / share (optional)** — Save the wheel configuration; share a link to a pre-built wheel.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**How random is the spin?**</summary>

Uses crypto.getRandomValues — same cryptographic-grade RNG used for security applications. Outcomes are uniform across segments to within statistical noise.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Why use a wheel instead of just random.choice?**</summary>

Visible randomness builds trust. When others see the wheel spin, they accept the outcome more readily than "the algorithm picked you".

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I pre-rig the outcome?**</summary>

No. The result is determined at spin time from the RNG; no setting to favour a specific segment outside of explicit weights.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is the weighted wheel still random?**</summary>

Yes — weights set the probability of each segment, but each spin is still randomly drawn from those probabilities. Equal weight = uniform.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I save my wheel?**</summary>

Yes, locally. Persisted in browser storage so the same wheel reopens next session. Export JSON to share or back up.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is this gambling?**</summary>

For non-money decisions, no. For gambling-related uses, check local laws — randomness alone does not make a wheel gambling, but money + outcome does.

</details>

## Tips

- Weight only when there's a real reason — equal weights are fairer and avoid suspicion.
- For draws-without-replacement (e.g. picking 3 winners), enable "remove on spin" to avoid duplicates.
- Project the wheel for classroom or team use — visibility is half the appeal.
- Use sound sparingly; a brief click on landing is enough, continuous music is distracting.
- Limit segments to ~12 for readability; more than that, group into categories or use a different tool.
