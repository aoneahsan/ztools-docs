# meeting-cost-calculator

A meeting cost calculator multiplies meeting duration × number of attendees × average hourly cost (salary + overhead) to produce a single dollar figure showing what the meeting actually costs the organisation, providing a hard data point against which to challenge unnecessary or oversized meetings. The ZTools Meeting Cost Calculator runs entirely in the browser, supports per-attendee salary input (or a flat average), accounts for fully-loaded cost (typically 1.3-1.5× base salary for benefits + overhead), and includes a live "ticker" mode that increments cost in real time during the meeting.

## Use cases

### Manager justifying smaller meetings

Recurring weekly all-hands: 25 people × 1 hour × $80/hr loaded = $2000 / week, $104k / year. Concrete number changes the conversation about whether the meeting needs all 25.

### Pre-meeting framing

Show the cost figure on the agenda slide. "This 90-min meeting costs ~$1500 — let's respect that." Behaviour change measurable in tighter agendas and earlier endings.

### Quarterly meeting audit

Sum the calculated cost of every recurring meeting on a team's calendar. Often surfaces 30-50% of meetings that can be cancelled, shortened, or made async with no loss.

### Pitching async-first cultures

Hard cost numbers anchor the case for async-first work — same coordination, fraction of the cost.

## How it works

1. **Enter headcount** — Number of attendees (use the actual count, not the invite list — calendar accept rates run 70-80%).
2. **Enter average salary** — Per-attendee or aggregate. Default: median engineer ~$120k base; manager ~$180k; senior leader ~$250k+. Adjust to your org.
3. **Apply loading factor** — Fully-loaded cost = base salary × ~1.3-1.5 (benefits, taxes, overhead). Conservative: 1.3x; HR-standard: 1.4x.
4. **Enter duration** — In minutes. Includes prep / context-switching cost (often 10-20 min per meeting on top of scheduled time).
5. **Compute** — Total cost = headcount × hourly cost × duration ÷ 60. Live ticker option for during-meeting awareness.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Why include overhead in the rate?**</summary>

Salary alone underestimates real cost by 30-50%. Benefits (health, retirement, payroll tax), facilities, equipment, and HR overhead are the same employee's cost just less visible. Use 1.3-1.5x for honest figures.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Should I include preparation time?**</summary>

Yes when it's significant. A 30-min meeting that requires 30 min of prep has 2x the visible cost. Tracker mode counts only the meeting itself; mental overhead can be added.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is this just shaming culture?**</summary>

No — it's data. Meetings that produce decisions worth their cost stay. Meetings that don't get cancelled or compressed. Cost transparency turns intuition into data.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What about meetings without dollars (volunteer, school)?**</summary>

Replace dollars with hours. 25 attendees × 1 hour = 25 person-hours of opportunity cost. Same accountability, different unit.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Are status meetings worth it?**</summary>

Often not. The same status, written async, costs ~10% of the meeting time and is searchable later. Cost calculator usually proves the case.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I reduce cost without cutting needed meetings?**</summary>

Smaller invite list (only decision-makers + 1-2 informers), shorter time-box (30 min default beats 60), better agendas (decisions vs discussion), async pre-reads.

</details>

## Tips

- Run the calculator on your standing recurring meetings first — they accumulate cost most invisibly.
- Show the cost number on the agenda slide. Humans respond to concrete numbers more than abstract "let's be efficient".
- Default new meetings to 25 or 50 minutes (Parkinson defeats), not 30 or 60. Buffer time prevents back-to-back fatigue.
- Track meetings cancelled / shortened over a quarter — concrete savings in dollar-hours, not vague "we cut meetings".
- For external meetings (sales, customer), the cost is the same. Salary loading still applies; treat customer time with equal respect.
