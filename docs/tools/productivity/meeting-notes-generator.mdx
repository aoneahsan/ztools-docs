# Meeting Notes Generator

A meeting notes generator produces a structured recap document covering the standard sections every meeting should leave behind — attendees, agenda, decisions, action items with owners + dates, parking lot, next steps — replacing free-form notes that are unreadable a week later with a consistent format that makes meetings searchable and actionable. The ZTools Meeting Notes Generator runs entirely in the browser, takes meeting metadata (title, date, attendees) + bullet inputs and outputs a clean, sectioned recap in markdown, with a one-click copy for pasting into Slack, email, or a doc.

## Use cases

### Weekly team meeting

Recurring 30-min team meeting. Same template each week; consistent format means anyone joining mid-quarter can read prior weeks and catch up.

### Project kickoff

New project meeting with cross-functional attendees. Notes capture decisions (scope, deadlines, owners), risks, and parking lot — defining the project officially.

### 1:1 conversations

Manager-IC weekly 1:1s. Notes anchor recurring threads (career goals, current projects, blockers) over weeks; both parties prepare against the running notes.

### Customer / sales call recap

Post-call: decisions, customer concerns, next steps with dates. Sent to internal team within an hour while context is fresh.

## How it works

1. **Enter meeting metadata** — Title, date, time, attendees, optional location / video link.
2. **Set agenda** — Bullet list of topics covered. Sets the structure for the recap.
3. **Capture decisions** — Each decision as one line: "We will [action] because [rationale]." Decisions, not discussion — discussion belongs elsewhere.
4. **Capture action items** — "[Person] will [action] by [date]." Owner and date are mandatory; without them, action items rarely happen.
5. **Generate recap** — Tool produces markdown with all sections. Copy-paste into your team's notes destination.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Should I take notes during the meeting?**</summary>

Yes — but lightly. Capture decisions and actions live; full prose can be filled in within an hour after. Trying to transcribe everything degrades attention.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Who should take notes?**</summary>

Rotate — everyone gets the cognitive lift of summarising. Permanent note-taker often becomes a bottleneck and a passive-aggressive role.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How long should notes be?**</summary>

20% of meeting length's worth of words. 30-min meeting → ~150-200 word recap. Longer is rarely read; shorter loses fidelity.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What if there are no decisions?**</summary>

That itself is data — a meeting without decisions is often a meeting that did not need to happen. Note that explicitly.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Should action items have due dates?**</summary>

Yes, always. "By Friday" or "by next sync". Without dates, they slip silently; with dates, they create accountability.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How do I make notes findable later?**</summary>

Consistent format + standard tagging (project name, date in title). Searchable by free-text in any doc tool. Inconsistent notes hide.

</details>

## Tips

- Capture decisions, not discussion. Discussions go in working docs; meeting notes capture conclusions.
- Send the recap within 24 hours. Latency erodes the artifact's usefulness; same-day is best.
- Tag attendees on action items. Visual ownership matters; "@john to ship X by Friday" is harder to ignore than "John to ship X".
- Maintain a "parking lot" section for off-topic items — keeps the meeting focused without losing ideas.
- Review last meeting's actions at the start of the next — accountability anchor.
