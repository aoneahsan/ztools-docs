# Collage Maker

A collage maker combines multiple photos into a single composite image using a chosen layout — grid (2x2, 3x3), freeform overlapping, or pre-built templates (story, polaroid, mosaic) — useful for social-media posts, family photo prints, before/after comparisons, and travel recap shots. The ZTools Collage Maker runs entirely in the browser using HTML5 Canvas, supports JPG / PNG / WebP inputs, drag-to-position, drag-to-resize, customisable spacing and background color, and exports the final composition as a single PNG with no server upload.

## Use cases

### Instagram carousel hero

Combine 4 trip photos into one square collage; pair with 9 standalone photos in the carousel. Combined cover doubles preview impact.

### Birthday / anniversary card

Photo collage of memories printed on a card or share digitally. More personal than a single posed photo.

### Before / after grid

Renovation, transformation, fitness — side-by-side or 4-panel before/middle/after grid. Visual storytelling in one image.

### Mood board for design

Designers / creatives assemble inspiration images into a single collage for client review or self-reference.

## How it works

1. **Pick layout** — Grid (2x2, 2x3, 3x3, 4x4), freeform, polaroid, mosaic. Determines slot count and arrangement.
2. **Drop images into slots** — Each slot accepts one image; drag from your filesystem. Auto-fits to slot dimensions; click to reposition / zoom inside slot.
3. **Customise spacing + background** — Gap between photos (0-50px), background color (white / black / custom), corner rounding.
4. **Resize canvas** — Output size: square (1080x1080 for IG), portrait (1080x1350), landscape, or custom.
5. **Export PNG** — Canvas exports a single composite PNG; downloads to your device.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**How many photos can I combine?**</summary>

Practically up to 16-20 in a single collage. Beyond that, individual photos become too small to read. For huge sets, use multi-page collage tools.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Are the images uploaded?**</summary>

No — Canvas processes entirely in the browser. Images and output stay on your device.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I add text or stickers?**</summary>

Basic text overlay supported. For elaborate annotations, export the collage and add text in an image editor.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**What about aspect-ratio mismatches?**</summary>

Tool offers fit (letterbox), fill (crop), or stretch. Fill is most popular for grid layouts; fit for freeform.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Why does the export look slightly different from preview?**</summary>

Preview rounds dimensions for performance; final export uses exact pixel math. Differences should be minor (1-2px).

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I save my layout for reuse?**</summary>

Yes — save as a template; reload to apply same layout to different photos.

</details>

## Tips

- Match aspect ratios within a collage — mismatched aspect ratios with "fit" leave awkward letterbox bars.
- Use small consistent gaps (5-10px) for clean grids; larger gaps for stylised polaroid-style.
- Export at the destination's native size (1080x1080 for IG, 1200x630 for Twitter card) — saves quality after their compression.
- For storytelling, reorder photos chronologically; for visual balance, alternate light/dark or zoom levels.
- Always inspect the export at 100% — small misalignments visible only at full size.
