# text-to-handwriting-converter

Text-to-handwriting converts typed input into realistic handwritten-style output rendered onto a paper-backed Canvas — useful for personal notes, journaling exports, art projects, school assignments rendered as draft, or simply giving digital text a more human feel. The ZTools converter uses real handwriting fonts (Caveat, Homemade Apple, Indie Flower, Permanent Marker, Sacramento, Shadows Into Light, Patrick Hand and many more from Google Fonts) layered over selectable paper backgrounds (lined, grid, blank, yellow legal pad). All rendering is browser-side; the page never sees your text.

## Use cases

### Personal journaling exports

Type your journal in a fast app, then export as handwritten-style PNG to print or share — keeps the digital convenience but feels more personal than printed Arial.

### Letters / thank-you notes

For people who can't hand-write (injury, accessibility) or whose handwriting is illegible. Print and sign manually for the personal touch where it matters.

### Artistic/design mockups

Designers use handwriting-style text in invitations, scrapbooks, posters, social posts. Faster than commissioning custom hand-lettering.

### School assignments draft

See how a hand-copied version would look before doing the final by hand. Useful for length estimation in handwritten essays.

## How it works

1. **Paste text** — Paragraphs render across multiple lines; word wrap respects paper width.
2. **Pick a handwriting font** — From a curated set: Caveat (casual), Homemade Apple (notebook), Indie Flower (rounded), Permanent Marker (bold) — 12+ included.
3. **Pick paper background** — Lined ruled paper, grid, blank, yellow legal pad, aged parchment. PNG layered behind text.
4. **Tune ink + slant** — Ink color (default deep blue/black), pen weight, slight letter-spacing/angle randomisation for realism.
5. **Export** — Save as PNG (single page) or PDF (multi-page if text exceeds one page).

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>**Will this fool a teacher?**</summary>

Probably not — fonts repeat letterforms, while real handwriting varies every letter. Use as a layout draft or for stylistic effect, not for academic dishonesty.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Can I upload my own handwriting font?**</summary>

Yes — drop a .ttf/.otf file and the renderer uses it. Pair with Calligraphr-style services if you want to build a font from your actual handwriting samples.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Why does it look "too perfect"?**</summary>

Real handwriting has ink variation, micro-spacing, and angular drift. Increase the "humanise" slider (slight rotation/spacing randomisation per letter) for more authentic output.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**How much text fits per page?**</summary>

About 250–400 words per A4 page at default font size, depending on font and ruled-paper line spacing. Longer text auto-paginates in PDF export.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Does it support non-Latin scripts?**</summary>

Some handwriting fonts cover Latin, Cyrillic, basic Greek; CJK and Arabic handwriting fonts are sparse. Check the font's Unicode range before relying on it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>**Is the text uploaded anywhere?**</summary>

No — Canvas renders entirely client-side. Your text never leaves the browser.

</details>

## Tips

- Mix two handwriting fonts (one for headings, one for body) for variety; pure single-font handwriting screams "fake".
- Use ruled paper backgrounds for journal/letter feel; blank for cards; grid for math/notes.
- Slight ink-color variation per paragraph (e.g. dark blue → black → dark blue) adds realism.
- For print, export at 300 DPI minimum; screen-only is fine at 96 DPI.
- Pair with a paper texture overlay (subtle grain) for an aged look in design projects.
